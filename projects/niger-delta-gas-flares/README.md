## Project Overview

The Niger Delta is one of Africa’s most important oil-producing regions and one of its most environmentally sensitive landscapes. Decades of oil and gas production have exposed mangrove forests, wetlands, protected areas, agricultural land, and nearby communities to multiple environmental pressures.

Gas flaring is one of the most visible environmental consequences of petroleum production in the region. However, evaluating its spatial distribution can be difficult because flare sites are widely dispersed across both onshore and offshore areas.

This project uses satellite remote sensing, spatial analysis, and Google Earth Engine to communicate where gas flaring occurs and to support the visual assessment of flare locations in relation to surrounding landscapes.

---

## Decision Problem

Environmental researchers, conservation organizations, government agencies, and local stakeholders need accessible tools for answering questions such as:

- Where are gas-flaring sites concentrated in the Niger Delta?
- Which parts of the region have the greatest spatial concentration of flare activity?
- Are flare sites located near environmentally sensitive landscapes?
- Which communities, wetlands, mangrove forests, or protected areas may require closer investigation?
- How can satellite observations support environmental monitoring where field information is limited?

The application converts geospatial data into an accessible interactive map that can support environmental screening, research communication, and stakeholder engagement.

## Study Area

The study focuses on the Niger Delta region of southern Nigeria.

The region contains extensive:

- mangrove forests;
- freshwater and coastal wetlands;
- river systems and creeks;
- agricultural landscapes;
- oil and gas infrastructure;
- coastal settlements;
- protected and conservation areas; and
- offshore petroleum-production zones.

Its ecological importance and concentration of petroleum infrastructure make the Niger Delta an important location for satellite-based environmental monitoring.

## Environmental Relevance

Gas-flare mapping can contribute to a broader understanding of environmental pressures within petroleum-producing landscapes.

Potential applications include:

- identifying locations for targeted environmental assessment;
- examining flare proximity to mangrove and wetland ecosystems;
- supporting protected-area monitoring;
- guiding field-data collection;
- developing environmental baseline assessments;
- communicating pollution concerns to stakeholders;
- supporting environmental justice research; and
- prioritizing locations for further air-quality or ecological investigation.

The application provides spatial evidence for screening and exploration. It does not, by itself, establish causation between a flare site and a specific ecological or health outcome.

## Limitations

This application should be interpreted as an exploratory environmental-monitoring and communication tool.

Important limitations include:

- satellite-derived flare observations may not detect every flare;
- detection may be affected by sensor resolution, atmospheric conditions, observation timing, and flare intensity;
- the presence of a mapped flare does not directly quantify local exposure;
- proximity does not prove ecological damage or human-health impact;
- flare activity may change after the observation period represented by the data;
- some offshore or low-intensity flare sites may be underrepresented; and
- field observations and official operational records remain important for validation.

The application should therefore support, rather than replace, detailed environmental assessment, emissions monitoring, regulatory investigation, and community-level field research.

---

## Responsible Interpretation

The mapped locations represent spatial observations or indicators of gas-flaring activity. They should not automatically be interpreted as evidence that a particular operator or facility has violated an environmental regulation.

The application is most appropriately used to:

- identify spatial patterns;
- develop research questions;
- prioritize environmental monitoring;
- support transparent communication; and
- guide more detailed investigation.
